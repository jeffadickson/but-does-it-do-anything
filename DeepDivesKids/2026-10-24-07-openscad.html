Meet the idea

OpenSCAD makes 3D shapes from short programs rather than mouse drawing.

Imagine this: design a treasure box in 3d. What would you make first?

How it works

You combine solid shapes with operations such as union and difference to add or remove pieces.

Try predicting: What changes when you give the program a different instruction or number?

Real examples—not guesses

OpenSCAD's official manual and examples show boxes, cylinders and constructive-solid-geometry modeling.

That's a documented example from the project's own published material. It does not establish that the software powers NASA missions, professional games, or Dad's projects.

Our invention challenge

Make a miniature rocket, a hockey-puck stand, or a tag for a gear bag; these are projects to try, not proven deployments.

A possible connection to Dad's work is testing ideas one step at a time, as engineers do with simulations, code, and careful experiments.

Try it!

Paste into OpenSCAD; press Preview. A smaller box is subtracted to make a tray.

difference() {
  cube([50, 35, 20]);
  translate([3, 3, 3]) cube([44, 29, 18]);
}

Challenge: Change one number or direction, predict the result, and record what happened. If this is pseudocode or a partial library example, use the linked official environment or tutorial.

What makes it tricky?

Excellent for exact, repeatable designs; organic or hand-sculpted shapes are less convenient.

Can we use it?

OpenSCAD itself is GPL-2.0-or-later licensed; model-file distribution and included dependencies have separate considerations. The lesson does not need OpenSCAD installed to read.

Ask an adult before installing software, publishing creations, or sharing personal information.
