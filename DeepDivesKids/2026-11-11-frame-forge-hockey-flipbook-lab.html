Today's creative challenge

Can eight pictures make one amazing save?

Imagine Quinn winds up for a shot. Reagan is ready in goal. Freeze the play. Move the puck a tiny bit. Freeze it again. When the pictures flash by, your brain sees a moving play.

Artists call this frame-by-frame animation. In stop-motion, artists actually move puppets and photograph them one frame at a time. In our lab, JavaScript draws pictures instead of taking photos.

Predict · Create · Replay
🎞️

Two kinds of movie magic

Stop-motion: Move a real puppet → photograph it → move it again → take another picture.

Digital flipbook: Put drawings at slightly different positions in a sequence → play them in order.

Important: This is a digital frame animator, not a camera-based stop-motion application.

How it works

How can still pictures move?

One frame is one picture. A still image does not move by itself.

Make one small change. Put the puck a little farther along in the next picture.

Play the pictures in order. Your visual system combines rapid changes into apparent motion.

Adjust the timing. Faster playback means more pictures shown each second, but smooth motion also needs enough different positions.

⏱️ The mathematical secret

time = number of frames ÷ frames per second

Eight frames at 4 frames per second make a 2-second movie.

Eight frames at 8 frames per second make a 1-second movie.

Professional films can display 24 frames each second. Some drawings are held for two display frames. Our eight-frame experiment makes each decision visible.

Evidence versus imagination

Who really uses animation like this?

🎥 Aardman and Wallace & Gromit

In a June 2025 behind-the-scenes article, Aardman described planning a film with an animatic (timed storyboard), building physical props, and using phones to capture a stop-motion film a frame at a time.

Verified real use: Aardman's production account.

🧵 LAIKA and Coraline

LAIKA and the Museum of Pop Culture explain that Coraline used puppet animation and describe shooting on ones (a change each of 24 frames per second) compared with twos (a change every other frame).

Verified real use: LAIKA's Coraline exhibit.

🖍️ Blender's Grease Pencil

Blender supports digital frame-by-frame drawing and onion skinning, which shows pale neighboring frames to help artists judge motion. Our lab makes a simpler version of this idea.

Verified capability: Blender's official manual.

Off-screen maker mission

Make a real paper flipbook

  1. Get eight sticky notes or small sheets of paper.
  2. Number the pages 1–8.
  3. Draw the same hockey net in the same place on each page.
  4. Draw a puck a little closer to the net each time. Let the goalie block it near the end.
  5. Stack the pages, hold one side and flip with your thumb.

🔬 One-variable experiment

Predict: Will smaller changes between pictures look smoother?

Test: Draw a second eight-picture flipbook with smaller steps.

Observe: Compare both at approximately the same flipping speed.

Explain: A movie is a series of separate observations, presented in order.

Coder corner

How do eight numbers become motion?

One short JavaScript pattern does most of the magic. These values record the puck's horizontal location in eight drawings:

const positions = [205,285,365,450,540,632,688,645];
let frame = 0;
function drawNext(ctx) {
  ctx.clearRect(0,0,900,460);
  ctx.fillStyle = '#152d4d';
  ctx.beginPath();
  ctx.arc(positions[frame],255,13,0,Math.PI*2);
  ctx.fill();
  frame = (frame+1) % positions.length;
}
const ctx = document.querySelector('#rink').getContext('2d');
setInterval(() => drawNext(ctx),250); // 4 pictures/second

This fragment is a simplified example for a page containing a canvas named rink. It shows a moving dot on a blank canvas. 🧠 Connection to Dad's engineering

A simulator like VeloSim examines a system one step at a time. An animation keeps track of a frame number; a circuit simulator keeps track of simulation time. Both replay ordered changes. This is an analogy, not an actual hardware simulator.

🏒 Connection to hockey

Look at individual pictures of a pretend goalie save. How does the goalie line up with the puck? Real coaches sometimes review real slow-motion video, too. This toy draws imagined positions; it does not measure actual athletes.

Director's badge

Three mystery questions

1. Eight pictures at four pictures per second last how long?

Two seconds. Eight divided by four is two.

2. Are the pale shapes on the ice real extra players?

No. Onion-skin ghosts let artists see neighboring frames.

3. Does our studio photograph real puppets?

No. It draws with code. In stop-motion, real objects are photographed at slightly different positions.

Bonus quest: Make the puck go straight in frames 1–4, then change direction in frame 5. Is it a deflection? How would you animate a goalie save?
